About the Book

The Room Without Windows

A contemplative parable about prayer, silence, hiddenness, and the slow reshaping of faith beneath visibility and performance.

A story for those who have prayed loudly and quietly

The Room Without Windows follows Elior, a man living in a city where faith is spoken constantly and confidently. Prayer fills the streets, public spaces, and daily rhythms of life. Words become proof of certainty. Silence becomes unfamiliar.

As Elior grows increasingly unsettled by the distance between performance and honesty, he discovers a small room hidden within his home, a room without windows, without spectacle, and without demand.

Inside that silence, prayer slowly changes.

What begins as discomfort becomes unlearning. What begins as interruption becomes attention. Elior’s faith moves away from certainty, repetition, and visible devotion toward something quieter, more hidden, and more enduring.

“The door is open.
It always has been.”

This book is written for readers who have wrestled with unanswered prayer, religious performance, exhaustion, hiddenness, and the longing to remain faithful without needing to prove anything.

Rather than offering formulas or conclusions, the story invites stillness, reflection, and presence. The chapters unfold slowly and intentionally, allowing silence itself to become part of the experience.

The Room Without Windows is not a book about escaping the world.

It is about learning how to remain within it honestly.
